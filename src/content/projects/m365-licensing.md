## Problem and goal

In October 2024, I overheard that Arizona Community Physicians was considering moving its Microsoft 365 licensing from its reseller to buying direct from Microsoft. Nobody had asked me to look at it. I wanted to know whether the move would actually save money before the decision was made, and whether the existing licensing was being used efficiently. Microsoft no longer included Teams in its enterprise licensing, so buying direct would have meant adding it back as a separate per-user charge.

## Stakeholders

- Leadership and the IT department were considering the switch to buying direct.
- Every employee with a Microsoft 365 license was affected by the cost.
- I presented the findings at the IT team's weekly meeting the following Tuesday.

## Approach

- I built a spreadsheet in one evening, in under two hours.
- It showed the current cost through the reseller side by side with the cost of buying direct from Microsoft for 800+ active users.
- Because Teams was no longer included in enterprise licensing, a separate column added the $5 per user Teams charge.
- The organization wanted Teams available to everyone, even though only some users actually signed in to it. I included a count of the users who really used it, so the comparison was honest about both options.
- The reseller was also giving a discount of about $2 per license, which closed most of the gap in a Teams-for-everyone scenario.
- I reviewed license assignments and found that licenses from terminated employees had piled up unassigned: over 100 E1 and over 100 E3 licenses that were paid for but not in use.
- The organization was locked into a year-long term with the reseller, so those licenses could not be dropped immediately. The renewal was a couple of months out, so I put the counts in the spreadsheet for reducing quantities at that point.
- I brought the spreadsheet to the next weekly meeting and walked through it.

## Risks and rollback

The risk was in the decision itself. Switching to direct billing would have raised the yearly cost, and the increase likely would not have been noticed until a renewal a year later. Nothing was changed in the tenant. The analysis was read-only, so no rollback was needed.

## Results

- Staying with the reseller avoided more than $100,000 in added licensing cost every year compared with buying direct.
- The analysis took under two hours.
- The spreadsheet documented over 100 unassigned licenses of each type, with the recommendation to reduce quantities at the next renewal.
- The team had not been aware of the Teams change before the meeting.
